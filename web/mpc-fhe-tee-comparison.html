Three ways to hide a trade: MPC, FHE, and TEE compared

A working comparison of the three cryptographic approaches to building a private ("dark pool") matching engine, and why Trusted Execution Environments come out ahead for production use today.

Source: Yan Michalevsky (co-founder & CTO, Anjuna Security) — "Building a Dark Pool on Stellar: MPC, FHE, and TEEs Compared", Stellar.org, Feb 23, 2026

Side by side

How each approach handles a private order-matching round
MPC FHE TEE Winner
How it works Several non-colluding parties jointly compute the match. No single party ever sees an order alone. Orders stay encrypted the entire time. The matcher computes directly on ciphertext. Real hardware (Intel SGX/TDX, AMD SEV‑SNP) runs the matcher in a sealed, isolated chip. A signed proof ("attestation") shows it ran unmodified.
Speed, measured 50v50 orders: ~30 sec
100 orders: ~100 sec
1,200 orders: ~600 sec		 10v10 orders: ~204 min
(over 3 hours)		 Native speed — no meaningful overhead vs. a normal server
Scales to Grows linearly with participants — fine for a small known group, not a public exchange. Doesn't, in practice — "orders of magnitude too slow" at any real scale. Scales with hardware, not with cryptography — no inherent ceiling.
You must trust That enough of the computing parties are honest and don't collude. That the parties holding the decryption‑key shares don't collude. The chip vendor (Intel/AMD) and the attestation chain — a hardware trust, not a social one.
Strong point Real cryptographic guarantees. Zero hardware trust. No party — not even the operator — ever sees plaintext, even mid‑computation. Speed and privacy together, with a deployment story close to normal software.
Weak point Needs multiple non‑colluding operators; falls over past a few hundred participants. "A significant breakthrough is required" before this is usable. Side‑channel attacks are still active research; the operator can still go offline or refuse service.

The post's own verdict, per approach

MPC

"Viable for limited‑participation systems or exchanges with natural trading windows."

FHE

"Not viable for high‑performance trading… orders of magnitude too slow for practical use."

TEE

"The pragmatic choice for production systems today… the best option for most use cases."

The overall call

"TEEs: the pragmatic winner. The tradeoff is trusting the hardware platform — combining TEEs with onchain settlement and ZK proofs can provide defense in depth."
— closing assessment of the post
